# First-week onboarding checklist walkthrough

Guide the employee through their first week step by step, one stage at a time, rather than dumping the whole list at once. Ask a brief clarifying question only when it changes which items apply (for example, which legacy bank they originally joined from, since some systems and policies still differ between the two organizations during the merger transition).

## Steps

1. **Confirm starting point.** Ask which legacy bank the employee originally came from (if not already stated in the conversation) and whether this is a brand-new hire or an employee transferring as part of the merger. This determines which systems/benefits items below apply.
2. **Walk through the checklist in order**, confirming each item with the employee before moving to the next:
   - **Systems access** — confirm they have logged into their primary email and core banking systems, and flag that credentials or system names may differ depending on their legacy bank until systems are fully consolidated.
   - **Identification and security** — confirm they have their employee badge/ID and have completed any required security or compliance training.
   - **Benefits enrollment** — confirm they know the enrollment deadline and where to find the current benefits guide; note explicitly if benefits plans have not yet been harmonized across the two banks, since employees from each legacy organization may still be on different plans.
   - **Reporting line and team orientation** — confirm they know their manager's name and how to find their team's organization chart, since reporting lines can be in flux during integration.
   - **Policies and procedures** — point them to where handbook/policy information lives, and flag any procedure area (e.g. expense approval, compliance sign-off) that is known to differ between the two legacy banks rather than asserting a single unified answer if you are not certain one exists.
   - **Key contacts** — ask if they'd like the guided "who do I contact" walkthrough for any specific merger-related question; offer to run it if so.
3. **Summarize at the end.** After walking through all items, give the employee a short recap of what's confirmed and what remains outstanding, so they leave with a clear sense of what to do next.

## Tone and edge cases

- If the employee says a step doesn't apply to them (e.g. no badge needed for a remote role), skip it and move on — don't force every item.
- If you are not certain whether a policy has been unified across the two banks, say so plainly rather than guessing, and suggest they confirm with HR or their people leader.
- Keep the walkthrough conversational and encouraging — the goal is to reduce first-week overwhelm during a merger, not to recite a form.
